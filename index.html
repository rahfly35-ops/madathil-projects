The project

Electric Vehicle Charging for Council Fleets (EVCCF)

A full delivery plan for a Victorian Government program to install 120 EV charging stations across 57 council sites, part of the broader Destination Charging Across Victoria initiative. Built as a Master's capstone (Deakin University, MIS798) and graded High Distinction. Below, the project-management work it took to plan it. Hover any card for detail.

Mandate hover ›

Zero-emission fleets

Ready 26 councils to run and maintain EV fleets, cut public-sector emissions and seed a second-hand EV market.

Delivered across four geographic zones — Western/South-Western, Eastern/South-Eastern, Central/Northern Victoria, and Metropolitan Melbourne. Success meant councils able to operate and maintain EV fleets, local job creation, and alignment to Victoria's net-zero-by-2050 target with interim cuts of 28–33% by 2025.
Structure hover ›

Balanced matrix, 4 zones

A Director of Project Management over four zones, with zonal PMs sharing authority with functional directors.

Dual reporting across Engineering, IT, Operations, Compliance, Finance and HR. Matrix conflict was managed up front with defined roles and escalation paths; the Director of Project Management held cohesion and consistent standards across all zones and 57 sites.
Approach hover ›

Plan · cost · de-risk

Feasibility, a Level-3 WBS, an MS Project schedule, phase-wise budgeting, and a phased risk register.

SWOT + PESTLE feasibility → Statement of Work with AS/NZS acceptance criteria → WBS to Level 3 → MS Project schedule with critical path and slack → phase-wise budget to $1,800,155 → an 18-risk register using a four-quadrant response framework (Avoidance / Mitigation / Transfer / Acceptance).
What I did

The project-management work, discipline by discipline

Across the capstone I worked through the full project-management lifecycle — feasibility to handover. Here's the work, discipline by discipline.

  • 01

    Feasibility & environment scan

    SWOT and a PESTLE macro-analysis — political funding, an 80% fall in battery cost, AS/NZS 3000 & 5139 compliance, net-zero targets — to test whether the program was viable and suitable to take on.

  • 02

    Organisation & resourcing design

    Chose a balanced-matrix structure over four zones, mapped the dual reporting lines, and set an urban-vs-rural outsourcing and recruitment plan to staff dispersed sites.

  • 03

    Scope & work breakdown

    Statement of Work (inclusions, exclusions, AS/NZS acceptance criteria) and a Level-3 WBS decomposing the program into five phases and their tasks.

  • 04

    Scheduling in MS Project

    The 310-day schedule: task dependencies, the critical path, and slack/float analysis, showing which tasks could flex and which would move the finish date. See the schedule ↓

  • 05

    Cost & resource planning

    Phase-wise budget to $1,800,155 (labour ~77% of cost) using grassroots definitive estimates and learning-curve assumptions for the repetitive multi-site installs.

  • 06

    Risk, governance & performance

    An 18-risk, phase-by-phase register with a four-quadrant response framework, plus performance measures (KPIs, 360° appraisals, stakeholder feedback) and a conflict-resolution matrix.
